Plate 5 — Two units, one fix, and the geometry that ruins it

A bearing from one Orb is a line. Two bearings are a position — but only as good as the angle they cross at. The third panel is the honest one: what three stations can and cannot tell you.

GDOP
Panels A and B use the same bearing uncertainty. Only the geometry changes. That is the point worth making out loud: a fix can be bad without any measurement being bad, and the operator needs to be told which situation they are in. The solver's trust flag requires GDOP below 10 for exactly this reason.
Two stations
Two rays that are not parallel always intersect somewhere, so a two-station fix is always perfectly self-consistent — including when one bearing is corrupted. Reporting a residual there would invite the reader to treat silence as corroboration, so the solver withholds it deliberately rather than reporting a comfortable zero.
Three stations
A triangle finally makes disagreement visible, but it cannot attribute it. Drop any one of the three and the remaining pair meets exactly, so three mutually incompatible explanations each fit their own evidence perfectly. This is a real limit of the method, not of the implementation.

Plate 6 — The deployed configuration

The case open, the lid flat, the array upright. Every geometric claim the direction-finding rests on is visible here as a physical arrangement.

§5.1
This drawing is the answer to "why is it a case with a lid that opens flat?" The flat lid is not packaging convenience — it is what puts the three elements in a horizontal plane, and a horizontal plane is what makes azimuth unambiguous. The enclosure follows from the physics rather than the other way round.
Orientation
The apex pointing at the body rather than away from it buys two things at once, and neither was luck: one element in the case's shadow instead of two, and two outboard feed runs that are mirror images of each other and therefore matched in electrical length without trimming — which is what the roughly 6 mm phase-match constraint needs.
Scope
Proportions are illustrative, not dimensioned. The 17.6 cm baseline is the real design value at 851 MHz; everything else on the plate is drawn for legibility. This is a 700/800 MHz configuration and the plate says so on its face.

Conceptual and pitch-quality, not procurement-ready. Plate 6 in particular is an illustration of an arrangement, not a mechanical drawing of a product — no case has been selected, measured, or fitted.
