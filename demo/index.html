P25 Orb A proposed ARC Edge add-on — proof of concept RX-only, unlicensed tier

When a P25 trunk saturates, officers press the button and nothing goes out. The trunk knows. Nobody else does. P25 Orb listens to that trunk and tells the on-scene commander what never got through.

Tab 1 — open first

RF Environment

What the receiver hears: raw P25 control-channel signalling, no interpretation. This tab owns the clock and drives the other one.

 Tab 2 — open second

Command Feed

The same events as the commander is shown them: running digest, two-tier alarms, and a ranked view of what needs attention now.

 Instead of the two above — for a video call

Presenter View

Both live tabs framed side by side in one window, so a screen share shows the Command Feed reacting the moment the traffic happens rather than making the audience take that on trust. Same pages, same clock — they still talk to each other through the frames. The zoom control trades visible area for stroke weight, which is the right trade when the far end is watching compressed video.

Where you see a dashed panel. Several things in this proof of concept are specified in the design documents but not built — receiver sensitivity reporting, multi-unit cooperation, the map layer. Rather than leave them out and describe them from the stage, they appear on the relevant page as dashed panels marked not built, each naming the requirement it stands for. Everything not marked that way is working code.

Running the demo

  1. Open RF Environment in one tab and Command Feed in a second. Put them side by side. Presenting over a video call instead? Open Presenter View and skip this step.
  2. Press Play on the RF Environment tab. It drives both tabs; the Command Feed has no timer of its own.
  3. Space plays and pauses, R resets. The numbered buttons jump to any of the seven beats for rehearsal.
  4. The whole sequence runs 2 minutes 52 seconds at 1×. There is a 2× option for rehearsal.
What is real and what is simulated. The detection, synthesis and alarm engine is real working code, and the AI classifications were genuinely produced by Claude Haiku 4.5 and cached so the presentation needs no network. The radio events themselves are a scripted timeline, because no P25 receiver hardware exists for this demo. ARC Edge's own path-selection is represented narratively, since that is Orb Aerospace's own intellectual property and not ours to implement.
